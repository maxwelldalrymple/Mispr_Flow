# Meeting notes: telling people apart, and faster live text

**Branch:** `speaker-id`

## Problem (from the user's test with a multi-person YouTube meeting)

- Speakers weren't recognised as the same person. The old spectral "voice signature" found **8 people in a 4-person meeting**.
- Live text was slow. Previews ran every 0.8 s on the same single thread as the final text, at about 1 s each with the large model, so they starved the final text.

## What changed

- **Voice fingerprints and cosine similarity.** Each stretch of "them" speech gets a 256-number WeSpeaker ResNet34 embedding via sherpa-onnx, compared by cosine similarity with each known person's running average.
  - The same person means similarity ≥ **0.60**.
  - Speech under 1.0 s joins its neighbour.
  - Only clear speech (≥ 1.5 s) can start a new person or refine a voice.
  - At most 8 people.
  - If the model can't load, the old signature is used.
- **Split at speaker changes.** Final chunks use Whisper's timed segments. Each segment of 1 s or more gets a speaker, short ones ("yeah") join their neighbour, and adjacent same-speaker segments merge. One chunk can now come back as several lines (`chunk_text` gains `last`; the app counts a chunk as done on its last line).
- **Fast live previews.** Previews run on their own thread with `ggml-base.en` (about 0.15 s, versus about 1 s with turbo). The turbo model only does final text.
- **Models.** Both new models are downloaded on the first meeting (not at first run), with pinned SHA-256:
  - `ggml-base.en.bin` (148 MB), from Hugging Face; the hash matches HF's published hash;
  - `wespeaker_en_voxceleb_resnet34_LM.onnx` (26.5 MB), from the sherpa-onnx GitHub release.
  - `sherpa-onnx` (Apache-2.0) was added to requirements.

## Measurements

**Model check on a real meeting.** AMI EN2002b, 5 min, 4 people, a single far-field mic, ground-truth labels.

| | same person | different people |
|---|---|---|
| WeSpeaker cosine similarity (turns ≥ 1.5 s, ≥ 90% one speaker) | 0.75 ± 0.07 | 0.48 ± 0.10 |

Clustering those clean turns at 0.60 finds **4 people (correct), with 93% of time assigned to the right cluster**.

**Chunk-level clustering on the same 5 min** (55 app chunks, count-weighted):

| | speakers found (truth 4) | purity | coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| old spectral signature | 8 | 76% | 62% |
| embeddings, threshold 0.60 | **4** | 73% | **87%** |

Time-weighted, purity is about 64–71% whether units are chunks or Whisper segments, and offline re-clustering didn't beat online. The limit is audio that mixes voices: overlap and backchannels in this far-field recording. The model itself separates well on clean turns.

**Live replay** of 2 minutes of the same meeting in real time through `MeetingWorker`: finals at each cut, previews every 0.8 s. The "before" row ran the new splitting code without the preview thread or embeddings, so its preview queueing matches the old design. A 5-minute run against the exact previous commit was started and stopped on request, so it has no result.

| | final text after a phrase ends | live preview lag | previews delivered |
|---|---|---|---|
| shared queue, large model for previews | median 2.07 s, p90 2.53 s | median 0.49 s, p90 1.55 s | 96 of 139 |
| **separate preview thread, base.en** | **median 1.27 s, p90 1.57 s** | **median 0.14 s, p90 0.27 s** | **139 of 139** |

Speakers in those 2 minutes: shared queue 8 found, purity 65%, coverage 48%; new 3 found, purity 57%, coverage 89%. This early in a meeting, two of the four voices are still merged.
