# Voice app switcher

**Branch:** `speaker-id`

## What it does

1. Hold the **app switcher key**.
2. The widget records and shows "Say an app to switch to".
3. Say an app ("Chrome", "open the terminal", "switch to Slack") or one of its nicknames, then let go.
4. The app comes to the front, launching if needed. The widget shows "→ Google Chrome".

- **Teach a nickname by voice:** "set nickname Scooby Snacks to Chrome", "nickname C for Chrome", or "set the nickname for Terminal to T". The nickname is saved to `settings.json`, and the app's Settings list refreshes at once (`settings_changed` event).
- **Settings → General → App switcher key** (off by default). Click it and press:
  - a single key (F5);
  - one side of a modifier (press and release Right ⌥);
  - a modifier combo (hold ⌃⌥ and let go);
  - modifiers plus a key (⌥S).
- fn, Esc, hands-free keys and the dictation key itself are refused. "Turn off" clears it. When it's on, a **Nicknames** list appears: add a nickname and pick an app, or remove one with ✕.
- **Matching** (`mispr/apps.py`) tries, in order:
  1. a nickname;
  2. an exact name;
  3. the name with spaces removed ("face time" → FaceTime);
  4. a near-exact prefix ("photo" → Photos);
  5. a word of the name ("chrome" → Google Chrome);
  6. any prefix;
  7. a close-sounding name ("sapari" → Safari).

  Running apps win ties. A single letter only works as a nickname. With no good match it plays the error sound and shows "No app called “…”".
- **Privacy:** apps are found by listing the app folders on disk and asking macOS what's running. Nothing goes online. The spoken audio is wiped right after and never saved to history. It uses raw Whisper text: no cleanup model, so it's faster.
- **Robustness:** with a modifier combo held, pressing any other key (a ⌘⌥T shortcut) cancels the switch. A quick tap does nothing. Modifiers still reach apps, while a single trigger key and its key-up are swallowed. Dictation (fn) keeps working alongside it.
